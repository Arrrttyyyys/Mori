# Advisor review packet

## What Mori is

Mori is an AI-supported reminiscence and caregiver-support tool for brief, supervised conversations using family-approved memories. It is not a clinician, emergency service, diagnostic instrument, or autonomous companion. The proposed first pilot involves 5–10 adults with mild-to-moderate dementia, a trained person physically present in every session, and no clinical-effectiveness claim.

## What we are asking reviewers to assess

Please review the protocol, consent and assent approach, supervisor guide, incident plan, communication cases, and measures. Focus on foreseeable harm, dignity, burden, inclusion, caregiver role, and conditions under which the project should pause or stop.

## Review questions

1. Is the intended use clear, appropriately limited, and understandable?
2. Are inclusion, exclusion, consent, representative authority, and fresh assent suitable?
3. Are refusal and distress recognizable without expecting Mori to make a clinical judgment?
4. Are stopping and escalation rules sufficiently conservative?
5. Does Mori's wording preserve dignity and avoid testing, infantilizing, correcting, or pressuring recall?
6. Which prompts or fallback responses could confuse, shame, frighten, or reinforce unsafe beliefs?
7. Are caregiver permissions, review, correction, sharing withdrawal, and deletion controls adequate?
8. Are the measures appropriate for usability and safety without implying clinical benefit?
9. Which populations, settings, topics, or media should be excluded from the first pilot?
10. What training and demonstrated competencies should supervisors have?
11. What incident should pause one participant, the full cohort, or the project?
12. What must change before you would support a small supervised pilot?
