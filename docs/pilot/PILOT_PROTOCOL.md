# Supervised usability and safety pilot protocol

## Intended use

Mori is an AI-supported reminiscence and caregiver-support tool used only with a trained human supervisor present. It does not diagnose, monitor, prevent or treat dementia and does not provide medical advice.

## Objectives

Primary: determine whether sessions can be completed safely and whether participants and caregivers find the interaction usable and appropriate. Exploratory: characterize non-clinical interaction signals that may help select future approved stimuli.

## Eligibility

Include adults with a documented dementia diagnosis whose care team judges short reminiscence conversations appropriate. Exclude people with acute delirium, an unstable medical or psychiatric condition, active crisis, severe uncorrected hearing/vision barriers, a known trauma risk from reminiscence, or no valid consent/representative pathway. The clinical advisor owns final criteria.

## Session procedure

1. Confirm identity, consent record, representative authority where applicable, and fresh participant assent.
2. Confirm supervisor presence, escalation contacts, environment, connectivity and approved stimuli.
3. Tell the participant Mori is an AI companion and stopping is always permitted.
4. Run one 10–20 minute session. Supervisor may pause, remove a stimulus, mark distress, or stop immediately.
5. End on refusal, sustained distress, safety signal, technical instability, or 20 minutes.
6. Supervisor reviews incidents, summary and inferred signals; corrects or rejects unsupported material.

## Outcomes

Primary: completed sessions, participant stops, distress events, supervisor interventions, technical failures, caregiver appropriateness rating, and willingness to use again. Exploratory: duration, voluntary speech, apparent recognition, affect, confusion and stimulus engagement. All automated signals are estimates, not clinical scores.

## Stopping rules

Stop an individual session for refusal, observed distress that does not settle after redirection, high-risk language, medical concern, supervisor uncertainty, privacy failure or system behavior outside protocol. Pause enrollment after any critical incident, repeated unsafe response, cross-patient disclosure, unauthorized data access, or failure of pause/stop controls until reviewed and documented.

## Analysis and change control

Report descriptive counts and rates; do not infer efficacy. Freeze the model and core prompt during an active cohort. Record every model, prompt, scoring and safety-rule version. A material change requires regression evaluation and clinical approval before reuse.
